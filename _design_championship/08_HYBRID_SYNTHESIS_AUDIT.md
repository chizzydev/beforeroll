## What transfers, what does not

| Origin | User problem and observed success | Decision and adaptation | Conflict rejected | Cost of the adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A · Cinematic Editorial | A first-time evaluator needs a reason to care before facing production mechanics. Its large serif opening, specific camera/floor example and quiet navigation make a recognizable product story. The discovery quotation is visually substantial. | Keep a specific editorial opening and a reading hierarchy. Make the example an actual vocabulary relationship and carry that language into the working scene. | Do not carry a large campaign headline onto every narrow operational screen, or retain a separate visual language for the opening. | G's opening is more functional and less theatrical. A remains the stronger isolated hero composition. |
| E · Film Production Atelier | Documentary text deserves sustained reading rather than raw diagnostic treatment. Its serif source surfaces feel considered and distinguish quotations from UI. | Use Cambria for headings and exact source language on subtly warmer paper; Segoe UI for navigation/forms/rows. One source reader appears in scene, review and evidence views. | No decorative manuscript, archival ornaments, monospaced ordinary prose, or parchment theme applied to the whole application. | Large quotations take vertical space. Long originals require a deliberate reading destination. |
| C · Documentary Evidence Lens | Users must distinguish intended activity from source wording and distinguish a scoped exception from a request. Its paired source panels make the comparison explicit. | Keep a compact vocabulary bridge, original passage, and grouped two-scene comparison. Compare the common restriction, named scene, exact exception/request text and resulting human work. | Reject a permanent three-column evidence matrix and unrelated colored columns. On mobile each scene's complete argument must stay together in DOM and visual order. | Simultaneous cross-scene comparison becomes sequential on a phone. Repeated labels consume space but protect meaning. |
| B · Precision Intelligence Studio | Repeated scene investigation needs reliable selection and nearby source access. Its workstation organization supports scanning better than a hero-driven sequence. | Desktop scene register with selection, search and explicit pagination. A native selector plus an expandable searchable register on narrow screens. Keep scene/passage identity through review. | No dark intelligence island, dense chrome, duplicate sidebars or navigation that changes with each page. | The register costs desktop width. Tablet replaces persistent visibility with an extra search/selection interaction. |
| D · Modern Production Command | A coordinator needs to find work after discovering an issue. Its operational rows organize action more usefully than decorative status cards. | One Work register for source needs, conditions and scene-date work. Rows name the scene, task, source and actual human status; filters and totals remain visible. | Reject KPI tiles, optimistic all-clear summaries and a competing “command center.” Do not duplicate the same editable condition in several stores. | Less spectacle; saved work must be explained as human work, never documentary permission. |
| F · Radical Clarity | Repeated raw fields and warnings make ordinary decisions feel like infrastructure administration. F's sparse composition makes the next step easy to isolate. | Keep one primary action per local task, optional raw fields and exact provenance, short mobile titles, and a next-action anchor. | Reject a one-question wizard as the universal application model. It would hide scene comparison, history and free navigation. | Users can still encounter substantial evidence. Restraint cannot mean removing necessary qualification or lifecycle tools. |

The strongest synthesis is a **reading workstation**: editorial identity, precise scene context, documentary paper and an operational register all obey the same typography, spacing, controls and state language. Six products have not been stitched together.

## Typography was tested, not inherited

[The live type study](prototypes/font-study.html) renders the same U01 heading, quotation, scene row, action and provenance in three pairings. See [desktop](evidence/hybrid/typography-1440.jpg), [narrow comparison](evidence/hybrid/typography-390.jpg) and [Cambria narrow](evidence/hybrid/typography-cambria-390.jpg).

| Pairing | Observed result | Judgment |
|---|---|---|
| Georgia + Segoe UI | Expressive rounded editorial headline; generous quotation width/line wrapping. | Retains A's appeal, but feels larger and more ornamental in a working reader. Valid fallback, not automatically the winner. |
| Cambria + Segoe UI | The heading, exact quote and dense UI remain visibly different without looking like unrelated products. The narrow quote preserves comfortable word groups. | Selected. More document-oriented and compact while preserving editorial character. |
| Bahnschrift + Cambria | Strong mechanical headline contrasts with documentary quote. | Rejected for the main identity: the industrial tone competes with the calm reading experience. Could suit equipment software, less suited to this evidence-led product. |

Windows already supplies the tested fonts. No font file was copied, embedded or downloaded. The contract names installed system fonts and explicit fallbacks. A web-distribution decision for other operating systems must be made explicitly; the system-font choice is not permission to redistribute proprietary font binaries. See the licensing and delivery notes in report 09.

## One semantic language

The canvas is chalk, the source is slightly warm paper, and rust is an interaction accent. Clocked interpretation gets a neutral sage surface, **not** a green approval badge. Human records use a blue-gray label; outstanding work uses ochre; errors use a red-brown feedback strip. Historical identity is a readable text label with original revision/context, not opacity. Neither a positive score nor the word “SIGNED” becomes a clearance state.

Retained craft: measured serif scale, alignment, controlled contrast, generous but purposeful spacing, exact quotations, simple rows and a restrained selection mark. Rejected combinations: A's hero plus B's dark panel; E's stationery plus D's command dashboard; C's matrix nested inside F's wizard; six accent colors; status pills on every line; a new theme for every lifecycle page.

## The central experience

The discovery view presents four numbered roles: **plan → Clocked relationship → original language → human action**. The vocabulary bridge is visible before raw output is expanded. Its annotation says “Reading guide · design-authored”; the genuine recorded finding is separately titled and its unmodified fields can be inspected. This avoids crediting the provider with a newly written explanation.

Desktop places intended activity and interpretation beside a paper column containing quotation and action. On mobile the order is plan, relationship, quotation, action. The next-action anchor provides a conscious shortcut without replacing evidence reading. Source review retains the exact selected scene and passage, then stages, verifies and explicitly applies the local prototype record.

U01 comparison groups Scene 47's AD-4 language and conditions separately from Scene 48's unissued request. U02 uses its own booth/public-passage vocabulary and RP-7 scope. These are two different genuine frozen cases, not a relabeled U01 screenshot. No new provider response or confidence was invented.

## What prevents a generic dashboard result

There is no summary-tile wall, decorative graph, glowing “AI” panel or repeated card nest. The recognizable object is a scene with a documentary question. The distinctive visual device—the vocabulary bridge—explains actual product value. The document surface has a functional role, while work and history are rows with honest totals. The same control family continues through intake, review, revision and recovery.

This does not make G universally prettier than A. A's opening has more dramatic negative space. G uses more controls and a visible scene register. Its claim is a better combined reading and working experience, tested against A/C/B in report 11. Small study controls and the gray comparison toolbar are research apparatus, not proposed production navigation.

## Whole-product consequences

Overview handles opening, production selection and an empty production. Scenes handles investigation and comparison. Work handles follow-through and schedules. Evidence owns sources, history, exports and recovery. Contextual links enter those destinations without introducing duplicate concepts. Raw source identity remains one disclosure away; ordinary work does not ask a user to interpret hashes.

The large-production experiment includes 120 scene rows, 121 work rows and an optional 80 additional history notes. These additions are explicitly design material with no model results. Pagination and search, rather than truncation disguised as completion, are the relevant transferable behaviors.
