## Adversarial penalties applied to G

G is not simply declared more beautiful. Its extra scene chrome and production controls reduce A's compositional purity; craftsmanship is scored **below A**. Its distinctive identity is scored equal to A, not higher. Larger scope and exact-context integration are harder to implement coherently, so implementation is scored below A/B. Tablet remains two-column reading with action below the initial viewport. Long narrow quotations remain expensive; compact metadata and smaller disclosure targets need accessibility refinement. The prototype's single long JS module and iterative CSS are exploration artifacts, not an approved production architecture.

Positive findings do not remove source uncertainty. Some genuine earlier recordings can contain a permission-oriented model finding even where the selected document does not settle scene scope. For example, the retained history includes Scene 48/paragraph 1's genuine permission-oriented finding. G must display it honestly as a historical interpretation alongside the exact language; it must not rewrite the record to make the product story cleaner or present it as current permission. Its fixed documentary comparison is a labeled design reading of the actual fictional source, not a fresh model verdict.

G's additional stress states were used to test internal consistency and feasibility. A/C/B were not penalized for lacking the extra 120-scene fixture or new recovery specimen; ranking relies on the common screens/tasks, with stress work supporting G's implementation-risk judgment. No feature-count bonus was awarded.

## Final ranking and rejected alternatives

1. **Editorial Intelligence Studio — primary recommendation.** Best overall reconciliation of immediate vocabulary discovery, documentary scope comparison, repeated scene navigation and source-bound action. Its documentary and operational views look like the same product. The browser exposed real defects; corrections were retested, not assumed.
2. **Cinematic Editorial — backup.** Strongest pure editorial impression, credible film identity, slightly simpler adoption. Prefer it if the owner values A's visual composition more than G's clearer working structure. It remains a serious contender, not a deliberately weakened control.
3. **Documentary Evidence Lens.** Excellent source-role and comparison premise; more document-centric and spatially expensive at tablet/narrow widths. Valuable principles have transferred, but the full composition does not lead the combined product.
4. **Precision Intelligence Studio.** Useful operational density and scene behavior; less distinctive identity and less immediate documentary storytelling. A credible workflow benchmark, not the selected identity.

D/E/F remain rejected as complete directions for the reasons in the original championship: operations can dominate the central discovery, atelier craft can become stationery, and radical simplification can hide the professional evidence workflow. Their useful principles are explicitly adapted in report 08. Their scores and artifacts are preserved.
